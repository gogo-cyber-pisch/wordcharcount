Frequently Asked Questions

Is this character counter completely free?

Yes, 100% free with no registration, no limits, and no hidden costs. Use it as often as you need.

Are spaces counted as characters?

We display both: characters with spaces and characters without spaces. Most social media platforms (like Twitter/X) count spaces as characters, so use the "with spaces" count for those limits.

Is my text stored or shared?

No. All processing happens locally in your browser. Your text is never sent to our servers, stored in a database, or shared with third parties. Your privacy is fully protected.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time is based on an average adult reading speed of 250 words per minute. Speaking time uses 150 words per minute, which is typical for presentations and speeches.

What is keyword density and why does it matter?

Keyword density shows how often a specific word appears in your text as a percentage of total words. For SEO, a density of 1–3% per keyword is generally recommended. Higher than that may be considered keyword stuffing by search engines.

How many characters are in a Twitter/X post?

Twitter/X allows a maximum of 280 characters per post. URLs always count as 23 characters regardless of their actual length. Our tool tracks this limit in real-time.

What is the ideal length for a meta description?

Google typically displays 150–160 characters for meta descriptions. We recommend staying under 155 characters to avoid truncation in search results.

How many words fit on one page (A4)?

A standard A4 page with 12pt font and normal margins fits approximately 250–300 words. A double-spaced page contains about 250 words, while single-spaced holds around 500 words.

Does this tool work on mobile?

Yes! Our character counter is fully responsive and works perfectly on smartphones, tablets, and desktop computers with any modern browser.

Can I use this for other languages?

Absolutely. Our tool correctly counts characters in any language, including those using special characters, accents, umlauts, or non-Latin scripts like Chinese, Arabic, or Japanese.
